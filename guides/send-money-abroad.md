When you send money across borders, you rarely pay just the "fee" you see on the screen. The real cost hides inside the exchange rate — and it is usually the bigger half of the bill.

## The hidden cost: the rate margin

Banks and many transfer services quote you a rate worse than the mid-market rate (the midpoint between buy and sell prices you see on Fxverter or Google). The difference is a margin of typically **1–4%** — sometimes much more on exotic corridors. On a 10,000-unit transfer, a 3% margin silently costs you 300 units, usually more than any advertised fee.

## Compare the total received, not the fee

The only number that matters is **how much arrives**. For any provider, calculate:

- The fee they charge, plus
- The gap between their rate and the mid-market rate

Multiply both into the destination currency and you get the true cost. A provider with "no fee" and a 4% margin is far more expensive than one with a visible $5 fee and a 0.3% margin.

## Three minutes of due diligence

- Check the **mid-market rate** for your pair right before you send — rates move
- Ask the provider for the **total amount in the recipient's currency**, including everything
- Compare at least two providers on the same day; margins differ wildly by corridor
- For large amounts, ask whether a **better rate tier** exists — many services negotiate above certain sizes
- Watch out for **promo rates** that apply only to the first transfer

## Timing and method

For most retail corridors, transfer-specialist services beat bank wires on cost, and speed is similar (minutes to a day). Avoid converting cash at airports — their margins are the worst in the industry. If the transfer is not urgent, watching the rate for a few days and setting an alert at a favorable level can be worth more than any fee discount.

## The one-line rule

**Ignore the fee, compare the received amount.** Everything else is marketing.
